Safe disposal guidance

  • Wrap used pads or diapers in paper or a disposal pouch before putting them in a bag.
  • Keep them separate from wet kitchen waste and dry recyclables, and follow your local municipal rules.
  • Never flush pads or diapers. Never burn them.
  • Empty solid waste from diapers into the toilet where local guidance allows.
  • Wash your hands well after handling waste.
In this prototype, no real collection takes place. In a real project, collection and treatment must be handled by authorised waste-management partners.

Donor human milk and milk banks

Information only. Nari Chakra does not collect, exchange or deliver breast milk.

Important: Breast milk donation must be voluntary and handled by qualified healthcare facilities. We do not support milk exchange, home collection or delivery between individuals, and there are no rewards or coins for donating milk.

Why donor milk matters

Some newborns, especially those born early or very small, cannot get enough of their own mother's milk. Screened and pasteurised donor milk from a milk bank can help protect these babies while they grow stronger.

What a milk bank does

A hospital-based milk bank screens donors, tests and pasteurises donated milk, stores it safely, and gives it to babies under medical guidance. These steps keep the milk safe.

Why hospitals only

Milk shared informally can carry infections or be stored unsafely. Working through a qualified facility protects both the donor and the baby.

General eligibility information

Each facility sets its own rules. Always confirm with the hospital before you decide.

  • The donor is usually a healthy lactating mother who has enough milk for her own baby.
  • Most facilities ask about medical history, medicines, and lifestyle, and may request a blood test.
  • Donation is a free, voluntary gift. It is never paid for or rewarded.
  • The facility's staff will explain how to express, store and hand over milk safely.
This page is general awareness only and is not medical advice. Please speak to a doctor or lactation consultant.

About Nari Chakra

Nari means woman. Chakra means wheel. Together: a wheel of care that keeps turning.

Our mission

To make responsible sanitary waste handling easier to talk about and easier to practise, and to connect that care with opportunities for women.

Objectives

  • Spread awareness about safe disposal of pads and diapers.
  • Show how a request, approval and reward system could work.
  • Promote reusable and recycled everyday products.
  • Share trustworthy information about hospital-based milk banks.

Environmental benefits

  • Less sanitary waste mixed into ordinary household garbage.
  • Safer work for people who handle waste.
  • More demand for reusable bags and recycled paper.
  • A habit of thinking about where things go after we use them.

Women empowerment goals

  • Open, stigma-free conversation about menstrual and infant care waste.
  • Possible livelihood and training opportunities in recycling and craft work.
  • Clear, reliable health information for mothers.

Limitations of this project

  • This is a college demonstration prototype. Nothing is connected to real waste-management or donation services.
  • Login, data and admin roles run only in the browser using LocalStorage, so they are not secure.
  • No real money moves. Reward coins and prices are demo values, and all statistics are sample data.
  • Product details and milk bank listings are examples, not verified facts.
